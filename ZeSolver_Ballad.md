# The Ballad of ZeSolver 1.0.0

Hear ye, hear ye, noble astronomers, gatherers of photons, cartographers of the heavens, and all other adventurers reckless enough to open a FITS file after midnight.

The Lord of Butter Knives has finally returned from his quest.

Admittedly, he was never the sharpest blade in the drawer.

He does not shine brightly in tournaments, cannot cut through armour or slay a dragon with a single blow, and occasionally requires several attempts merely to determine which end is the handle.

But the butter knife remains indispensable.

And against all expectations, this one has returned alive.

Along the road, he was forced to confront the terrible Dragon of Star Databases, an ancient beast whose scales were made of obscure binary files, gigantic catalogues, mysterious data formats, thousands of celestial tiles, and several gigabytes of information stubbornly refusing to remain where they had been placed.

Never could I have imagined that this quest would become so complex.

At the beginning, I naively believed that one merely needed to read a few stars, compare a few points, and proudly announce that the sky had been solved.

I was young.

I was innocent.

I had not yet encountered quads, hashes, geometric indexes, fragmented catalogues, paths containing spaces, interrupted downloads, persistent settings, or the countless creatures lurking within the depths of a graphical interface.

More than once, the Lord of Butter Knives believed his final hour had come.

He was even forced to summon a Mage.

This Mage, known to the elders as the Wizard, now appears upon the first launch. He guides travellers, inspects the available lands upon their disks, downloads the required celestial grimoires, verifies their integrity, and installs the ZeSolver Library without requiring the unfortunate user to master the secret rituals of developers.

Yet even the most powerful Mage could not defeat the Dragon of Catalogues alone.

The aid of the great Lord Han Kleijn had to be sought, creator of ASTAP and guardian of the HNSKY databases.

In his wisdom, he expressly granted our humble expedition permission to use and distribute the required data for non commercial purposes, provided that the proper acknowledgements and notices accompany them.

Without his assistance, the road would have been far longer and probably lined with the smoking remains of the Lord of Butter Knives.

Through this alliance, ZeSolver may now be used in two different ways.

The most cautious travellers may use ZeNear with an existing ASTAP database. ZeNear then relies upon the information already contained within the images to determine their position in the heavens quickly.

Those who wish to explore the entire kingdom may install the complete ZeSolver Library through the Wizard. They will then possess ZeNear for swift solutions and ZeBlind for those images that carry no useful clues and must be found within the immensity of the sky.

To use ZeBlind, downloading and installing the complete library through the Wizard is required.

This is not a small parcel that can be hidden discreetly inside a pocket.

It contains both the star catalogues required by ZeNear and the geometric indexes used by ZeBlind.

The good news is that, at this hour, the road is safe.

My GitHub repository now provides the code and the official databases required by ZeSolver. The Wizard can download the individual components, verify their integrity, resume interrupted transfers, and activate the library once the installation has genuinely succeeded.

In other words, the age when users were required to sacrifice a goat, modify three configuration files, and recite a forgotten command in an ancient language should now be largely behind us.

The repository may be found here:

https://github.com/tinystork/ZeSolver

A website named ZeSoftware will soon open its gates.

In time, it will gather the various projects of the great Ze family, together with their documentation, repository links, and download mirrors to assist travellers whenever the main roads become overcrowded.

For now, ZeSolver has been genuinely tested on Windows and Linux.

Automated messengers returning from the lands of Apple claim that the code should be capable of surviving there, but no human has yet completed the entire quest upon a real Mac.

Therefore, should a Lord of the Mac, a Lady of Apple Silicon, or any noble knight possessing a machine bearing the mark of the apple wish to attempt the adventure, it would be a tremendous honour.

Their testimony may allow us to declare officially that ZeSolver also functions within those distant lands, or perhaps reveal the existence of some macOS specific demon concealed behind a file path, a permission, or a mysterious multiprocessing mechanism.

Finally, as always, the graphical interface remains my Nemesis.

I can fight astrometric algorithms, dissect binary files, and spend entire nights pursuing a missing star through the depths of a NumPy array.

Yet moving a button by twenty pixels without awakening three unintended consequences remains a form of dark magic that sometimes exceeds my understanding.

All feedback is therefore welcome.

Should an option appear obscure, should a process feel unnecessarily complicated, should a sentence resemble an ancient prophecy translated from Latin by an exhausted goblin, or should you simply have an idea that could make the experience clearer and smoother, please do not hesitate to tell me.

ZeSolver was created for our community, and it will continue to evolve through your tests, your impossible images, your error reports, and your suggestions.

The next quest, however, promises to be even more perilous.

It is now time to return to the deepest chambers of the ZeMosaic code and integrate ZeSolver within them.

I confess that I face this prospect with some concern.

Light never enters those depths.

The functions are ancient, the dependencies move whenever no one is watching, and certain portions of the code still bear the marks of decisions made by a man who had absolutely no idea what he was building.

Unfortunately, that man was me.

Fortunately, J.A.R.V.I.S, Lord of Electrons, Master of Python, and tireless guardian against errors I have not yet had time to commit, has never failed me.

Thanks to him, to Lord Han, to the great works of ASTAP, HNSKY, and Astrometry.net, and to everyone who tested, encouraged, and submitted impossible images to solve, the Lord of Butter Knives may now return to the castle with his head held high.

The release of ZeSolver 1.0.0 has arrived.

The beast has been defeated.

The heavens stand open.

And for once, the butter knife has managed to cut something.
